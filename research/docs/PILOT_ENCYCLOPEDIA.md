## MOS-0001 — Great Mosque of Kairouan
**French:** Grande Mosquée de Kairouan · **Arabic:** الجامع الكبير بالقيروان · **Original:** جامع عقبة بن نافع (ar)

**Location:** Kairouan, Tunisia
**Founded:** 670 CE  
**Present structure:** Primarily ninth century (rebuilding begun 836)

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: A landmark of early Islamic architecture in North Africa, founded in the seventh century and substantially rebuilt during the Aghlabid period.
- FR: Un monument majeur de l’architecture islamique en Afrique du Nord, fondé au VIIe siècle et largement reconstruit à l’époque aghlabide.
- AR: معلم بارز للعمارة الإسلامية في شمال إفريقيا، تأسس في القرن السابع وأُعيد بناؤه إلى حد كبير خلال العهد الأغلبي.

### Historical interpretation

The early foundation dates to 670, while much of the visible architecture is ninth-century Aghlabid work.

### Architecture

The layout combines a porticoed courtyard, a hypostyle prayer hall and a monumental square minaret.

### Construction phases

- **670** [foundation; documented]: Friday mosque founded by Uqba ibn Nafi according to scholarly interpretation. Sources: SRC-KAI-SMART.
- **836 onward; ninth century** [reconstruction; documented]: Major Aghlabid rebuilding, followed by further ninth-century interventions. Sources: SRC-KAI-SMART, SRC-KAI-UNESCO.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P01-01 [documented]: A congregational mosque was founded at Kairouan in 670. (SRC-KAI-SMART)
- CLM-P01-02 [documented]: The mosque visible today is principally a ninth-century rebuilding. (SRC-KAI-SMART, SRC-KAI-UNESCO)
- CLM-P01-03 [documented]: The present plan includes a hypostyle prayer hall and a square three-storey minaret. (SRC-KAI-UNESCO)
- CLM-P01-04 [documented]: The mosque belongs to the UNESCO-listed Kairouan property, inscribed in 1988. (SRC-KAI-UNESCO)

### Heritage relationship

within_inscribed_property: Kairouan (499). The Great Mosque is within the inscribed city property, not a separately inscribed World Heritage property.

### Direct research sources

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Kairouan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/499) [SRC-KAI-UNESCO]
- [Smarthistory / Colette Apelian: The Great Mosque of Kairouan](https://smarthistory.org/the-great-mosque-of-kairouan) [SRC-KAI-SMART]

## MOS-0004 — Mosque–Cathedral of Córdoba
**French:** Mosquée-cathédrale de Cordoue · **Arabic:** مسجد قرطبة الجامع – الكاتدرائية · **Original:** Mezquita-Catedral de Córdoba (es)

**Location:** Córdoba, Spain
**Founded:** 786–788 CE (original mosque)  
**Present structure:** Multi-period: 8th–16th centuries

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: An exceptional multi-period monument preserving successive Islamic mosque phases alongside later Christian cathedral architecture.
- FR: Un monument exceptionnel associant plusieurs phases de l’ancienne mosquée islamique à l’architecture de la cathédrale chrétienne.
- AR: معلم معماري متعدد المراحل يجمع بين توسعات المسجد التاريخي وعناصر الكاتدرائية المسيحية اللاحقة.

### Historical interpretation

The original mosque dates to 786–788; major enlargements preceded its conversion into a cathedral in 1236.

### Architecture

Its famous double arches, hypostyle spaces and later Renaissance interventions belong to different historical phases.

### Construction phases

- **786–788** [initial_construction; documented]: Original congregational mosque built during Abd al-Rahman I. Sources: SRC-COR-OFFICIAL.
- **833–848** [extension; documented]: First substantial extension under Abd al-Rahman II. Sources: SRC-COR-OFFICIAL.
- **962–966** [extension; documented]: Expansion under al-Hakam II. Sources: SRC-COR-OFFICIAL.
- **991–994** [extension; documented]: Eastward enlargement under Almanzor. Sources: SRC-COR-OFFICIAL.
- **1236** [change_of_use; documented]: Consecration as a Catholic cathedral. Sources: SRC-COR-UNESCO.
- **1523–1599** [reconstruction; documented]: Major Renaissance interventions in the present building. Sources: SRC-COR-UNESCO.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P04-01 [documented]: The original mosque was constructed in 786–788 under Abd al-Rahman I. (SRC-COR-OFFICIAL)
- CLM-P04-02 [documented]: Large enlargements took place in the ninth and tenth centuries. (SRC-COR-OFFICIAL)
- CLM-P04-03 [documented]: The monument has functioned as a Catholic cathedral since 1236. (SRC-COR-UNESCO)
- CLM-P04-04 [documented]: Major Renaissance alterations occurred between 1523 and 1599. (SRC-COR-UNESCO)
- CLM-P04-05 [documented]: The Mosque of Cordoba was inscribed in 1984; the World Heritage property was extended in 1994. (SRC-COR-UNESCO)

### Heritage relationship

original_inscription_later_extended: Historic Centre of Cordoba (313). Mosque inscribed in 1984; property enlarged and renamed Historic Centre of Cordoba in 1994.

### Direct research sources

- [Cabildo Catedral de Córdoba: The history — Mosque–Cathedral of Córdoba](https://www.mezquita-catedraldecordoba.es/en/descubre-el-monumento/la-historia/) [SRC-COR-OFFICIAL]
- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Historic Centre of Cordoba](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/313) [SRC-COR-UNESCO]

## MOS-0003 — Jameh Mosque of Isfahan
**French:** Mosquée du vendredi d’Ispahan · **Arabic:** الجامع الكبير بأصفهان · **Original:** مسجد جامع اصفهان (fa)

**Location:** Isfahan, Iran
**Founded:** Early Islamic period (exact year unverified)  
**Present structure:** Multi-period, with major Seljuk elements

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: A living architectural record of Iranian mosque design, notable for its four-iwan courtyard and Seljuk domes.
- FR: Un témoignage majeur de l’évolution des mosquées iraniennes, remarquable par sa cour à quatre iwans et ses coupoles seldjoukides.
- AR: سجل معماري بارز لتطور المساجد الإيرانية، يتميز بصحن ذي أربعة إيوانات وقباب سلجوقية.

### Historical interpretation

The complex underwent successive transformations across many dynastic periods; its exact earliest foundation remains unconfirmed here.

### Architecture

The four-iwan arrangement, two domes and layered brick and tile decoration document architectural innovation over time.

### Construction phases

- **Early Islamic period** [early_foundation; uncertain]: An earlier mosque occupied the site; exact founding year not fixed by this source. Sources: SRC-ISF-UNESCO.
- **Seljuk period** [extension; documented]: Domes and four-iwan plan strongly associated with Seljuk expansion. Sources: SRC-ISF-UNESCO.
- **Later Islamic periods** [modification; documented]: Further additions and decorative programs through subsequent dynasties. Sources: SRC-ISF-UNESCO.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P03-01 [documented]: The monument illustrates approximately twelve centuries of Islamic architecture. (SRC-ISF-UNESCO)
- CLM-P03-02 [documented]: The four-iwan layout and two major domes are central Seljuk-era contributions. (SRC-ISF-UNESCO)
- CLM-P03-03 [documented]: The Jameh Mosque of Isfahan was individually inscribed on UNESCO’s World Heritage List in 2012. (SRC-ISF-UNESCO)

### Heritage relationship

individually_inscribed: Masjed-e Jāmé of Isfahan (1397). 

### Direct research sources

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Masjed-e Jāmé of Isfahan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1397) [SRC-ISF-UNESCO]

## MOS-0101 — Great Mosque of Djenné
**French:** Grande Mosquée de Djenné · **Arabic:** الجامع الكبير في جينيه · **Original:** Grande Mosquée de Djenné (fr)

**Location:** Djenné, Mali
**Founded:** Traditionally 13th century; not securely dated  
**Present structure:** 1906–1907 reconstruction

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: A major monument of Sudano-Sahelian earthen architecture whose present form dates from the 1906–1907 reconstruction.
- FR: Un grand monument d’architecture de terre soudano-sahélienne, dont l’aspect actuel résulte de la reconstruction de 1906–1907.
- AR: معلم بارز للعمارة الطينية في منطقة السودان والساحل الإفريقي، يعود شكله الحالي إلى إعادة البناء بين عامَي 1906 و1907.

### Historical interpretation

An earlier mosque is traditionally linked to the thirteenth century, but the date is not securely documented.

### Architecture

Earthen masonry, projecting timber supports and recurring community maintenance are key to its architectural identity.

### Construction phases

- **Traditionally 13th century** [early_foundation; traditional]: An early mosque is traditionally associated with the thirteenth century; not archaeologically established by these sources. Sources: SRC-DJE-SMART.
- **1834–1836** [reconstruction; documented]: Earlier replacement mosque documented in scholarly account. Sources: SRC-DJE-SMART.
- **1906–1907** [reconstruction; documented]: Present earthen mosque reconstructed. Sources: SRC-DJE-UNESCO, SRC-DJE-SMART.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P101-01 [documented]: The present Great Mosque was reconstructed in 1906–1907. (SRC-DJE-UNESCO, SRC-DJE-SMART)
- CLM-P101-02 [documented]: The monument uses local earthen building traditions and requires periodic maintenance. (SRC-DJE-UNESCO, SRC-DJE-SMART)
- CLM-P101-03 [traditional]: Its early thirteenth-century foundation is described as traditional or legendary rather than proven. (SRC-DJE-SMART)
- CLM-P101-04 [documented]: The UNESCO-listed Old Towns of Djenné property includes the mosque and has been on the World Heritage in Danger List since 2016. (SRC-DJE-UNESCO)

### Heritage relationship

within_inscribed_property: Old Towns of Djenné (116). Serial World Heritage urban property; on the List of World Heritage in Danger since 2016.

### Direct research sources

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre: Old Towns of Djenné](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/116) [SRC-DJE-UNESCO]
- [Smarthistory / Elisa Dainese: Great Mosque of Djenné](https://smarthistory.org/great-mosque-of-djenne/) [SRC-DJE-SMART]

## MOS-0017 — Sultan Mosque
**French:** Mosquée du Sultan · **Arabic:** مسجد السلطان · **Original:** Masjid Sultan (ms)

**Location:** Singapore, Singapore
**Founded:** 1824–1826 (original mosque)  
**Present structure:** Rebuilt 1925–1932; completion reported as 1928 or 1932

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: An emblematic Singapore mosque, first built in the nineteenth century and reconstructed with distinctive golden domes in the twentieth century.
- FR: Une mosquée emblématique de Singapour, fondée au XIXe siècle puis reconstruite au XXe siècle avec ses coupoles dorées caractéristiques.
- AR: مسجد بارز في سنغافورة، أُنشئ أول مرة في القرن التاسع عشر وأُعيد بناؤه في القرن العشرين بقبابه الذهبية المميزة.

### Historical interpretation

The first mosque dates from 1824–1826; institutional sources report different completion dates for its successor.

### Architecture

The present mosque is noted for its golden domes, minarets and Indo-Saracenic architectural influences.

### Construction phases

- **1824–1826** [initial_construction; documented]: Original mosque erected under Sultan Hussein Shah. Sources: SRC-SIN-NLB, SRC-SIN-NHB.
- **1925–1932; completion disputed** [reconstruction; disputed]: Replacement building designed by Denis Santry; museum says 1928, national library says 1932. Sources: SRC-SIN-NHB, SRC-SIN-NLB.
- **1975** [heritage_designation; documented]: Designated a Singapore national monument. Sources: SRC-SIN-NLB.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P17-01 [documented]: The first mosque was built between 1824 and 1826. (SRC-SIN-NLB, SRC-SIN-NHB)
- CLM-P17-02 [documented]: The National Museum collection dates the completion of the replacement to 1928. (SRC-SIN-NHB)
- CLM-P17-03 [documented]: The National Library Board dates final completion of the replacement to 1932. (SRC-SIN-NLB)
- CLM-P17-04 [documented]: The surviving mosque features Indo-Saracenic architectural influence and golden domes. (SRC-SIN-NLB)
- CLM-P17-05 [documented]: Sultan Mosque is a national monument designated in 1975. (SRC-SIN-NLB)

### Heritage relationship

national_monument: Singapore National Monument (not applicable).

- [National Library Board Singapore: Sultan Mosque — Singapore Infopedia](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=12ffc367-4687-4c85-beb6-b5779a42983f) [SRC-SIN-NLB]
- [National Heritage Board Singapore / National Museum: The Sultan Mosque, collection 1194747](https://www.roots.gov.sg/collection-landing/listing/1194747) [SRC-SIN-NHB]

## MOS-0102 — Mosque of Amr ibn al-As
**French:** Mosquée d’Amr ibn al-As · **Arabic:** مسجد عمرو بن العاص · **Original:** مسجد عمرو بن العاص (ar)

**Location:** Cairo, Egypt
**Founded:** 641 CE  
**Present structure:** Principally 20th-century reconstruction

### Visitor summary (EN / FR / AR)

- EN: Founded in 641 in Fustat, this historic congregational mosque survives largely through later reconstruction.
- FR: Fondée en 641 à Fostat, cette mosquée historique nous est parvenue essentiellement sous la forme de reconstructions ultérieures.
- AR: تأسس هذا المسجد التاريخي في الفسطاط سنة 641، ويعكس المبنى القائم اليوم عمليات إعادة بناء لاحقة.

### Historical interpretation

The Egyptian heritage authority dates the original foundation to 641 and identifies the visible structure principally as twentieth-century reconstruction.

### Architecture

Courtyard and arcaded prayer spaces evoke early Islamic spatial traditions, though the present fabric is much later.

### Construction phases

- **641** [foundation; documented]: Founded as the congregational mosque of the newly established Fustat. Sources: SRC-EGY-MTA.
- **20th century** [reconstruction; documented]: The building now standing primarily reflects twentieth-century reconstruction. Sources: SRC-EGY-MTA.

### Verified or explicitly qualified claims

- CLM-P102-01 [documented]: The mosque was founded in 641 under Amr ibn al-As. (SRC-EGY-MTA)
- CLM-P102-02 [documented]: Little of the original building survives; its present form chiefly reflects twentieth-century reconstruction. (SRC-EGY-MTA)
- CLM-P102-03 [documented]: The monument is situated in historic Fustat, now part of Cairo. (SRC-EGY-MTA)

### Heritage relationship

not_verified: not verified (not applicable). No UNESCO or equivalent designation is asserted in this pilot.
